# TALK — Textbooks Are All You Need (phi-1)

## Talks, Slides and Press Coverage

1. **Microsoft Research — "Textbooks Are All You Need" (publication page):** The official Microsoft Research publication page for the paper, describing phi-1 as a 1.3B parameter code model trained on textbook-quality data. ([link](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/publication/textbooks-are-all-you-need/))

## Q&A (answers from the paper)

These answers come from the paper itself, not from an interview. Direct quotes are marked with their section in the [arXiv paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.11644).

**Q1: Why did the authors focus on data quality instead of scaling up model size or dataset size?**
A1 (from the paper, Section 1): "Following the footsteps of Eldan and Li [EL23], we explore the improvement that can be obtained along a different axis: the quality of the data." The authors argue that standard code datasets contain many samples that are "not self-contained," consist of "trivial or boilerplate code," or have algorithmic logic "buried inside complex or poorly documented functions" (Section 2), making them inefficient for learning — like trying to learn from a bad textbook.

**Q2: What exactly is "textbook quality" data?**
A2 (from the paper, Section 2): "We conjecture that language models would benefit from a training set that has the same qualities as a good 'textbook': it should be clear, self-contained, instructive, and balanced." The training data consists of three components: filtered code-language data from The Stack/StackOverflow (~6B tokens, selected by a transformer-based classifier for "educational value"), synthetic Python textbooks generated by GPT-3.5 (<1B tokens), and synthetic exercises (~180M tokens).

**Q3: How much did finetuning on CodeExercises matter?**
A3 (from the paper, Section 2 and Figure 2.1): The base model phi-1-base (trained on CodeTextbook only) already achieved 29% HumanEval. Finetuning on the small CodeExercises dataset (~180M tokens) boosted this to 50.6% — a 22 percentage-point improvement from less than 200M tokens of exercise data. The authors note this finetuning also "unlocks many interesting emergent capabilities" not seen in the base model (Section 3).

**Q4: Did the authors check for data contamination with HumanEval?**
A4 (from the paper, Section 5): Yes. N-gram overlap analysis found only 4 HumanEval questions with 13-gram overlap, all false positives. More importantly, they performed aggressive data pruning — removing 40%+ of CodeExercises that were even vaguely similar to HumanEval problems — and the retrained model still achieved 45–50.6% HumanEval, outperforming StarCoder. The authors argue this "data pruning experiment is a fair way to evaluate performance."

**Q5: What are the limitations of phi-1?**
A5 (from the paper, Section 6): "phi-1 is specialized in Python coding, which restricts its versatility compared to multi-language models." It also "lacks the domain-specific knowledge of larger models such as programming with specific APIs or using less common packages," and "is less robust to stylistic variations or errors in the prompt." The authors note that GPT-3.5-generated synthetic data "has a high error rate," though phi-1 still achieves high performance despite these errors.

## Common Misconceptions

1. **"phi-1 proves small models are always better than large models"** — The paper does not claim this. It shows that for a specific narrow task (Python code generation), high-quality data allows a small model to compete with much larger ones. The authors explicitly list limitations: phi-1 is Python-only, lacks knowledge of less common APIs, and is less robust to prompt variations (Section 6).

2. **"The synthetic textbooks are just GPT-3.5 outputting random code tutorials"** — The paper describes careful prompt engineering for diversity: "we look for ways to inject randomness into the prompt in a way that gives rise to the generation of a diverse dataset" (Section 2.2). They targeted specific topics, audiences, and constraints to avoid the homogeneity that simple repeated prompting would produce.

3. **"phi-1's HumanEval performance is just from memorizing the benchmark"** — The contamination analysis (Section 5) directly addresses this. N-gram overlap was minimal (4 false positives at 13-gram). The data pruning experiment removed 40%+ of exercises similar to HumanEval and still achieved 45–50.6%. The authors also tested on 50 new unconventional coding problems (Table 2) where phi-1 scored 52 vs StarCoder's 51, confirming the performance generalizes.
