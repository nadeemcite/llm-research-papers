# TinyStories: How Small Can Language Models Be and Still Speak Coherent English? (TinyStories)
[![Kaggle](https://kaggle.com/static/images/open-in-kaggle.svg)](https://www.kaggle.com/code/nadymsazad/tinystories-small-language-models)

- **Paper:** [TinyStories: How Small Can Language Models Be and Still Speak Coherent English?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.07759)
- **Authors:** Ronen Eldan, Yuanzhi Li
- **Lab, year:** Microsoft Research, 2023 (arXiv preprint, May 2023)

## Summary

In May 2023, Microsoft Research researchers Ronen Eldan and Yuanzhi Li asked a simple but powerful question: how small can a language model be and still produce coherent English? Conventional wisdom held that models needed at least 125M parameters (like GPT-2 small or GPT-Neo small) to generate anything beyond a few coherent words, even after extensive training on large corpora like the Pile or Common Crawl. The authors suspected the problem was not the models but the data — large, diverse corpora overwhelm small models with too much information to process.

They created TinyStories, a synthetic dataset of short stories generated by GPT-3.5 and GPT-4 that use only vocabulary a typical 3-4-year-old would understand. Each story is 2-3 paragraphs with a simple plot. To ensure diversity, each story was prompted with a random combination of a verb, a noun, and an adjective from a ~1500-word vocabulary, plus random features (dialogue, plot twist, bad ending, moral value). They then trained models with as few as 1M parameters — or even a single transformer block — that produced fluent, grammatically correct, diverse stories with near-perfect grammar and demonstrated reasoning capabilities, far surpassing GPT-2 XL (1.5B parameters) trained on standard corpora.

They also introduced GPT-Eval, a new evaluation paradigm where GPT-4 grades generated stories as a teacher would grade student essays, scoring grammar, creativity, and consistency. This multidimensional evaluation overcame the limitations of standard benchmarks that require structured outputs.

This notebook trains a 4-layer Transformer (~8.3M parameters) on 10,000 TinyStories for 3 epochs on a Kaggle T4 GPU, achieving a test perplexity (a measure of how well a language model predicts text — lower is better) of 56.75 and demonstrating the training dynamics that the full paper builds upon.

## What Problem Does It Solve

Imagine trying to teach a child to write stories using a university library — thousands of books on every topic from quantum physics to medieval history. The child would be overwhelmed. But give them a shelf of simple storybooks with basic words and clear plots, and they will quickly learn to write their own.

That is exactly what TinyStories does for language models. Standard training data (Wikipedia, web crawls) is like the university library — it forces small models to learn language, facts, and reasoning all at once, and they fail at all three. TinyStories is the shelf of simple storybooks: it preserves grammar, vocabulary, and basic reasoning but strips away the overwhelming breadth, letting small models focus on learning to speak coherently.

## Core Idea

- **Synthetic data with controlled vocabulary:** TinyStories stories are generated by GPT-3.5 and GPT-4 using only words a 3-4-year-old understands, reducing the vocabulary and knowledge breadth that small models must master.
- **Diversity through random word combinations:** Each story is prompted with a random verb, noun, and adjective from a ~1500-word list, plus random features (dialogue, twist, bad ending), forcing the dataset to span the full vocabulary with rich combinations.
- **GPT-4 as evaluator:** Instead of structured benchmarks, GPT-4 grades stories on grammar, creativity, and consistency — a multidimensional evaluation better suited to open-ended text generation.

## Key Method Details

- **Dataset:** TinyStories, generated by GPT-3.5 and GPT-4. Stories use only vocabulary a typical 3-4-year-old understands. Each story is 2-3 paragraphs. A variant (TinyStories-Instruct) adds instruction-following: word lists, features, summaries, and prescribed sentences.
- **Architecture:** GPT-Neo based, with window size 256 and context length 512. GPT-Neo tokenizer restricted to the top 10K tokens. Models range from 1M to ~80M parameters, with 1-8 layers.
- **Training:** All models trainable on a single V100 GPU within 30 hours. Models are available on Hugging Face (TinyStories-1M/3M/9M/28M/33M/1Layer/2Layer).
- **Results:** A 28M-parameter model generates coherent multi-paragraph stories that surpass GPT-2 XL (1.5B) quality. Even 2.5M models produce coherent text. One-layer models (21M) capture factual knowledge. Grammar emerges before consistency, which emerges before creativity. Width is more important for factual knowledge; depth is more important for context-tracking.
- **GPT-Eval scores:** The 28M/8-layer model scores Grammar 9/10, Creativity 7/10, Consistency 9/10. The largest ~80M model reaches near-perfect grammar and consistency but falls short on creativity.

### Simplifications in This Notebook

- 4-layer Transformer with 256-dim and 8 heads (~8.3M parameters), instead of the paper's range of 1M-80M with GPT-Neo architecture
- Word-level tokenizer with 10K vocabulary, instead of the GPT-Neo subword tokenizer restricted to 10K tokens
- 10,000 stories from the validation split (the paper uses the full training set of ~2.7B tokens)
- 128-token block size instead of the paper's 256 window / 512 context
- 3 epochs with AdamW (lr=3e-4, weight_decay=0.1) in ~60 seconds on T4, instead of up to 30 hours on V100
- Perplexity and qualitative samples instead of GPT-4 grading

### What the Notebook Result Shows

On a Kaggle T4 GPU, the 8,312,320-parameter model trains in 61.6 seconds over 3 epochs. Training loss decreases steadily from 5.98 to 4.71 to 4.33. On 200 held-out stories, the model achieves a test loss of 4.04 and perplexity of 56.75 — it has learned meaningful word prediction from the TinyStories dataset. At this training scale (3 epochs on 10K stories), the model produces only short completions before predicting end-of-text; it has learned story structure and vocabulary but needs substantially more training (the paper trains for up to 30 hours) to generate multi-paragraph coherent stories.

These numbers are not comparable to the paper's GPT-Eval scores — they demonstrate the training dynamics and data pipeline at toy scale.

## Influence

TinyStories became a widely used benchmark and dataset for studying small language models. It directly inspired phi-1 (Gunasekar et al., 2023), which extended the "high-quality synthetic data" philosophy to code generation. The dataset is available on Hugging Face and has been used in numerous studies on scaling laws, interpretability, and training dynamics for small models. The GPT-Eval paradigm influenced subsequent LLM-as-judge evaluation approaches. About 52 citations ([OpenAlex](https://openalex.org/W4376652984), checked November 2026).
