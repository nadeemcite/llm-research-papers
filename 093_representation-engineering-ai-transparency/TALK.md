# TALK — Representation Engineering: A Top-Down Approach to AI Transparency (RepEngineering)

## Talks, Slides and Press Coverage

1. **Andy Zou — "Representation Engineering: A Top-Down Approach to AI Transparency" (October 2023):** Andy Zou presented the RepE framework in talks at various AI safety venues, covering the LAT method, honesty extraction, and representation control techniques. ([arXiv paper page](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.01405))
2. **Center for AI Safety — "Representation Engineering" announcement (October 2023):** The CAIS announcement describes RepE as an approach that "places population-level representations, rather than neurons or circuits, at the center of analysis" and shows applications to honesty, harmlessness, and power-seeking. ([link](https://www.safe.ai/blog/representation-engineering-a-new-way-of-understanding-models))

## Q&A (answers from the paper)

These answers come from the paper itself, not from an interview. Direct quotes are marked with their section in the [arXiv paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.01405).

**Q1: How is RepE different from mechanistic interpretability?**
A1 (from the paper, Section 2 and Appendix A): RepE takes a top-down approach, starting from high-level concepts and functions (like honesty, harmlessness) and working backward to find their representations. Mechanistic interpretability is bottom-up, starting from individual neurons and circuits. The paper argues that "RepE places population-level representations, rather than neurons or circuits, at the center of analysis." The appendix discusses that "mechanisms are flawed for understanding complex systems" and that representations may be the more appropriate unit of analysis.

**Q2: What is Linear Artificial Tomography (LAT) and why is it named that?**
A2 (from the paper, Section 3.1.1): LAT is named by analogy to neuroimaging (tomography). It consists of three steps: (1) designing stimulus and task, (2) collecting neural activity (hidden states), and (3) constructing a linear model (PCA). The paper says "similar to neuroimaging methodologies, a LAT scan is made up of three key steps."

**Q3: Does the reading vector really *cause* honesty, or just correlate with it?**
A3 (from the paper, Section 4.3.3): The paper demonstrates causality through the control experiments: "by directly adding the honesty reading vectors into its activations, we successfully control the model to output honest statements. Conversely, by subtracting the reading vectors from the activations, we can induce the model to lie." This bidirectional control is what establishes "a strong counterfactual effect."

**Q4: How many stimulus pairs are needed?**
A4 (from the paper, Appendix C.1): "Generally, our set will contain between 5 and 128 such pairs." The paper also notes that "the natural variation within random pairings often yields satisfactory results" and that the procedure is unsupervised by default.

**Q5: Why use the last token position for decoder models?**
A5 (from the paper, Section 3.1.1): For decoder models with next-token prediction objectives, "a natural position for collecting neural activity associated with concepts is the tokens corresponding to <concept> in the task template" or alternatively "the token immediately preceding the model's prediction (typically the last token)." The paper validates this empirically (Figure 5) and uses last-token by default.

## Common Misconceptions

1. **"RepE replaces mechanistic interpretability"** — The paper positions RepE as complementary, not a replacement. Appendix A discusses the relationship in detail, arguing they are different units of analysis (populations vs. neurons) that can coexist. The paper says RepE "draws on insights from cognitive neuroscience" rather than claiming to supersede circuit-level analysis.

2. **"The honesty direction is a single neuron"** — It is not. The paper explicitly contrasts population-level representations with individual neurons. The reading vector is the first principal component of a set of normalized pairwise differences across many stimuli — a population-level direction, not a single unit.

3. **"RepE only works on large models"** — While the paper uses LLaMA-2 (7B-70B), the method itself is model-agnostic. The LAT procedure works on any Transformer that exposes hidden states. The paper notes that "even unlabeled datasets, or datasets generated by the model itself can be effective in eliciting salient responses."
